# Gilded Table

**Philosophy:** The quiet authority of an object made for a room where people gather at night. Not a startup mark; a seal, a sign over a door, the brass fitting on a box that has been opened ten thousand times. The work should read as hand-drawn by an engraver and then cast in metal: every curve resolved, every weight balanced, nothing left to a default. It must look like it took weeks, by someone at the absolute top of their field.

**Space and form.** Circles and arcs dominate: the table, the ring, the seal. Four is the sacred number, four seats, four quarters, four cards, and it is expressed as symmetry you can feel before you count. Forms are closed and self-sufficient; a mark must survive at 16 pixels and at the size of a banner, so silhouettes are decided first and ornament is added only where it earns its place. Negative space is shaped as deliberately as the strokes.

**Color and material.** One metal, one light. Warm gold (#e2b659 into #f3d48c) as the body, never flat: a drawn gradient that suggests an object catching candlelight, with a darker gold (#8a6a2a) for engraved lines. The accent is the table's own blue glow (#4fc3ff), used once per mark as a gem, an edge, a single lit card, the way a jeweler sets one stone. Ground is near-black (#06080d / #0b0e15). No other hues.

**Scale and rhythm.** Line weights step in a strict ratio, hairline, medium, heavy, so engraved detail sits inside a bold outer silhouette. Letterforms are serif with authority (Gloock, Italiana, Crimson) or a thin tracked sans for secondary words; the word "CLUB" is always smaller, spaced wide, a whisper after the name. Repetition builds craft: ticks around a ring, bands on a crown, a card fan where each card is placed by a rule, not by eye.

**Composition and hierarchy.** Mark first, name second, everything else silent. Each lockup is built so the mark alone could stand as an app icon and the wordmark alone could sign a page. Meticulously crafted, painstakingly aligned, the product of deep expertise: every tangent intentional, every optical center corrected by hand, every glow restrained to the point of being felt rather than seen.
